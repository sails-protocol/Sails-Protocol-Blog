## The shift

A market can become more regulated and less accessible at the same time. Brazil has created a specific authorization framework for virtual-asset service providers; Europe has imposed MiCA requirements on service providers and certain stablecoins. These frameworks pursue consumer protection and market integrity, but they also force businesses to revisit capital, governance, distribution and operating costs. That does not prove that every closure was caused by regulation. It does change the economics of entry.

## The design response

Why should every wallet become a vertically integrated financial institution just to help people discover and negotiate opportunities? Sails proposes reusable coordination instead. Its protocol and SDKs are intended to separate trade intent, evidence, authorization and settlement from custody. The first product focus is P2P trading, not a claim that an entire financial stack already operates in production.

## A failure worth testing

A promising marketplace that cannot prove whether a bank transfer settled may strand both sides of a trade. A new interface cannot fix that uncertainty by changing a status label.

## What comes next

The next step is not a promise of regulatory immunity. It is to make the boundaries of each operator’s authority inspectable, test the claims made about the software and assess the legal obligations of each activity and jurisdiction.

## The strategic opening

An independent developer faces a different decision from a regulated custodian. Building another balance-holding intermediary may demand authorization, treasury controls and an operational organization. Building software that allows users to retain keys and interact with independent counterparties changes that starting point, but the business must still map every real service it performs. Sails is arriving during this reassessment, not because laws stopped applying to software, but because control over funds and control over coordination can be designed separately.

The test is practical: can two independently operated interfaces show the same offer without becoming its custodian, and can the trade reach a defensible conclusion after one party disconnects? Those are harder questions than a slogan about decentralization. They turn the opportunity into engineering requirements.

## The opportunity is a test, not a victory lap

The case for an open P2P coordination layer becomes persuasive only when an independent wallet and an independent marketplace can agree on the same trade without trusting a shared operator. A roadmap is not evidence of that result. For the beta, the decisive question is what can be reproduced when an order is contested, a network stalls, or a participant reconnects.

## Brazil: the companies behind the regulatory shift

The change is no longer theoretical. Coinext publicly attributed its orderly exit from retail crypto brokerage to the new minimum regulatory capital requirement: it said it did not meet the threshold and would not apply for authorization. Bity, previously branded Bitybank, also announced an orderly end to its brokerage, choosing not to allocate the capital and operating structure needed for authorization. Neither company described its exit as bankruptcy. Their statements matter precisely because these were established businesses, not hypothetical startups.

The impact is not uniform. Bybit announced a migration of eligible Brazilian customers to a Brazil-based entity. The BCB's Resolution 520 provides a transition framework for foreign operators, including a deadline and conditions for transferring operations and customers. Bitso has published a help-center notice about ending direct Brazilian retail service by October 30, 2026, with a [Mercado Bitcoin transition](https://support.bitso.com/hc/pt-br/articles/52896460737812-Detalhes-sobre-a-parceria-da-Bitso-com-o-Mercado-Bitcoin-no-Brasil). These are distinct responses: brokerage closure, local restructuring and a retail-service transfer.

The evidence supports a pressure toward consolidation, not yet a measured market-concentration ratio or proof that every international operator left. Sails' thesis is that independent wallets and marketplaces need interoperable coordination so the next generation of P2P products is not forced to recreate every custodial and institutional function. This architectural choice does not waive licensing for an operator that actually performs regulated services.
